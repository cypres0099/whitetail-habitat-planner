# Habitat lessons from the recordings

| Decision | What the recording contributes | How to test it on a farm |
|---|---|---|
| Which season has a forage gap? | In his four-parcel example LaPratt changes his interpretation as acorns and crops become available. An autumn map can go stale while its geometry stays accurate. [Land Types, 3:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVvuY4wU26Y&t=218s) | Walk the same oak edge and adjoining opening on repeated dates. Record mast on the ground, green browse and crop condition before assigning a seasonal purpose to a new plot. |
| Which edge is worth improving? | LaPratt links edge vegetation with browsing in the BowHunter Planet interview. Later he describes a grass-to-timber transition with little conspicuous sign. His two examples support inspecting the plants at an edge before interpreting it. [BowHunter Planet #360, around 7:56 and 25:54, publisher audio](https://audio4.redcircle.com/episodes/27c7a072-2abe-42bb-b71c-2cb869bd6cd2/stream.mp3) | Compare two short segments with similar terrain. Inventory usable browse, sight distance, evidence of use and whether mowing reaches them. Preserve an untreated segment during a native-strip trial. |
| Will a new plot interfere with existing farm traffic? | His diagram follows a person across the proposed field and back out. A planting footprint alone misses that round trip. [Entering and Exiting, 0:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zLuB5J2L51E&t=19s) | Trace both legs of ordinary work trips, including tractor turns and vehicle parking. Visit at leaf-off. Move or shrink a proposal if it pushes routine traffic across quiet cover. |
| What would justify expanding a pilot? | In a televised visit LaPratt describes watching how deer use an arrangement and changing it in response. This is a case example from his own ground. [Michigan Out-of-Doors #2115, 18:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u8qzd1za8Rg&t=1090s) | Choose an observable result before planting. Compare establishment, browse and repeated use against a similar untreated place. Keep observation effort comparable and record flood damage or crop rotation. |
| Should a field be divided? | The Deer IQ discussion examines how the shape of an opening changes sightlines within it. The speaker’s explanation of individual behavior is a design hypothesis. [Deer IQ field-design excerpt, 1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XbtaegUU2TQ&t=112s) | Stake a short test strip while preserving machine access. Check sightlines in both directions at animal height. Monitor vegetation establishment before creating a permanent division. |
| How much weight should a seed recommendation carry? | LaPratt introduces High Oil Corn as a product he is promoting. Its value as a record of his practice differs from its value as comparative evidence. [High Oil Corn, opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-3GFQLjAfus) | Start with the diagnosed forage gap and soil test. Compare an appropriate local crop or native planting with a small trial. Record cost, establishment and browse before buying for the whole field. |

The field tests in the right column are applications developed for this skill. Use site measurements and local conservation guidance when deciding whether to implement them.
