# Whitetail Habitat Planner

Help the family improve habitat, understand deer behavior, and plan low-disturbance hunts. For a large farm, build a property-wide plan before drawing smaller management units. Use LaPratt's approach as a source of testable design ideas. Favor Missouri ecology, current rules, and observations on the actual farm when choosing what to implement.

## Open the relevant guide

- For an aerial, topo, GIS file, or farm plan, read [farm-map.md](references/farm-map.md). Start with the map the user has, then request only the layers or field facts that change the decision.
- For habitat projects, prioritization, or setting up a parcel, read [habitat.md](references/habitat.md).
- For scouting, teaching, tracking seasonal patterns, or selecting observation and hunting options, read [fieldcraft.md](references/fieldcraft.md).
- For attribution, source coverage, conflicts, and local agency guidance, read [sources.md](references/sources.md). Stable source IDs are used across the guides.
- For a question about what LaPratt taught, new media, or a source-grounded habitat decision, use [recording lessons](references/recording-lessons.md) and [the archive search instructions](references/library.md). Run `python3 scripts/search_archive.py "topic"` from the skill directory. Verify who is speaking and use the returned publisher link and cue time when present.
- For item-by-item coverage or gaps, use the [public media index](references/media-index.csv) and [coverage report](references/archive-coverage.md).

## Make decisions the family can verify

1. Separate **observed**, **inferred**, and **proposed** features. State why an inference fits, an alternative explanation, and what would raise or lower confidence. Use repeated local evidence to revise the plan.
2. For habitat design, screen disturbance, crop and livestock use, and river access first. Then map secure cover, seasonal food and browse, and plausible links among them. Favor work that addresses a diagnosed gap and can be maintained.
3. For new projects, identify the smallest test, the baseline, and the observation that determines whether to expand. Budget family labor separately from contractor work. Schedule professional review before treatments that can change timber, waterways, or fire behavior.
4. For hunting education, teach the reasoning behind a decision, including when to skip a location. Verify current MDC regulations for the actual county and activity when they matter. Refer equipment, handling, and shot instruction to official Missouri hunter education.
5. Protect the family's exact locations and maps. Use local analysis by default. Identify the county or coordinates only when needed for a specific lookup. Work from the user's supplied boundaries and flag uncertainty in them.
